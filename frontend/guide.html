The guide

How to solve a Rubik's cube

Never held one before? Start at the top and go step by step — you will solve it with the beginner method in an afternoon. Already can? Pick a faster method below. Every move sequence here can be watched on a 3D cube.

Before you start

Start here

Five minutes on what the cube actually is makes every method easier.

Centres (1 per side, 6 in all) never move against each other. The centre decides a side's colour: the side with the red centre is the red side.
Edges (12) sit between two centres and have two colours.
Corners (8) have three colours.
So you are not moving stickers around — you are moving pieces into place. A white-red edge can only ever go between the white and red centres.

Reading the moves

Each letter is a side, seen from where you are: Up, Down, Right, Left, Front, Back. A letter alone means a quarter turn clockwise, as if you were looking straight at that side. ' (say “prime”) means anticlockwise, and 2 means a half turn.

Hold it still

Pick a front and keep it facing you. Moves only make sense if the cube does not turn in your hands.

Algorithms are recipes

A sequence like R U R' U' is a fixed recipe that does one job. You do not need to understand it — just do it exactly, in order.

Extra letters

M turns the middle slice (the same way as L). A lower-case letter like r turns that side and the middle slice together.

Comparing the methods

They all solve the same cube. They differ in how many moves, how much to memorise, and how they feel.

Choosing

Which one should I learn?

New to the cube

Learn the Beginner method first — every other method builds on what it teaches.

Want to get fast

Move to CFOP next. Start with the 2-look last layer shown here; it is the most documented method by far.

Like thinking, not memorising

Try Roux. Fewer moves and fewer algorithms, more planning. Great for one-handed solves.

Like smooth, steady turning

Try ZZ. After the first step you only turn three sides, with no cube rotations.

Getting better

Practice tips

Slow is smooth

Turn slowly without stopping rather than fast with pauses. Speed comes by itself.

One step at a time

Practise the step you find hardest on its own. Scramble, solve up to that step, then repeat it.

Learn with the app

Put your cube into the solver and pick Beginner or CFOP — it shows every turn of that method on your own cube, step by named step.
