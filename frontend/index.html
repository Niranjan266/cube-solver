How to read the turns

Two minutes and you will never need this page again.

1. Hold the cube the same way all the way through

Pick one face to be the front and keep it facing you for the whole solve. Never turn the whole cube — only the layers. Every instruction below assumes that grip.

Front the side facing you
Back the side facing away
Up the top layer
Down the bottom layer
Right the right-hand column
Left the left-hand column

2. Scanning: which face to show, and which way up

The app asks for the six faces in this order. The picture shows the face to point at the camera, and the arrow shows which side of the cube must be pointing upwards while you do it.

3. Every turn, with a picture

A letter on its own means turn that side a quarter turn clockwise, as if you were looking straight at it. The blue squares are the pieces that move, and the arrow is the way they go.

4. The mark after a letter

R turns the right side one way, R\' (say "R prime") turns it the other way — those are the twelve pictures above. R2 means turn it twice, half a turn, and for that one the direction does not matter:

5. The bit that trips everyone up

"Clockwise" always means looking straight at that face. So for the back face a clockwise turn looks anti-clockwise from where you sit, and for the left face the column comes down at the front rather than up. That is exactly why the pictures above are drawn the way you see the cube, and why the app puts a live arrow on the 3D model. Follow the arrow and you cannot get this backwards.

6. If you lose your place

Press ◀ to step back, or drag the slider. The 3D cube in the middle always shows exactly what your cube should look like right now — compare it with the one in your hands.

7. Why so few turns?

Shortest finishes in about 21 turns and never more than 30. The cube looks scrambled almost to the end — that is normal, everything falls into place at once. Switch to Learn to watch it solve layer by layer instead: many more turns, but every stage has a goal you can see.
